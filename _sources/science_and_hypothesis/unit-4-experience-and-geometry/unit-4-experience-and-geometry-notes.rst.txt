1. Exact Algebraic Model of Stellar Parallax Conventionalism (``Fig. 8``)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

Chapter V § 3 (FR p. 93 / EN pp. 72–73; :ref:`Concordance PC.11 <concordance>`) anticipates what twentieth-century philosophy of science termed **confirmational holism**: astronomical triangulation never tests pure geometry $G$ alone, only the conjunction $(G \wedge O)$ of geometry and optical propagation $O$.

In :func:`geometor.poincare.disk.construct_stellar_parallax_conventionalism` (``Fig. 8``), three astronomical stations $S_1(-3/5, -2/5)$, $S_2(3/5, -2/5)$, and $S_3(0, 3/4)$ inside $\mathbb{D}$ are joined both by Euclidean straight chords and by exact orthogonal circular arcs:

* Along the Euclidean straight chords $/S_1 S_2/$, $/S_2 S_3/$, $/S_3 S_1/$, the base-angle spread at $S_1, S_2$ is $s_{\mathrm{Euc}}(S_1) = \frac{529}{889} \approx 0.59505$ and the apex spread at $S_3$ is $s_{\mathrm{Euc}}(S_3) = \frac{62208}{79025} \approx 0.7872$, whose interior angles sum identically to $\pi$.
* Along the circular optical trajectories $(S_1 S_2)$, $(S_2 S_3)$, $(S_3 S_1)$ orthogonal to $\Omega$, every vertex angle strictly contracts ($\alpha + \beta + \gamma < \pi$, positive :term:`angular defect <defaut-angulaire>`). Yet the *identical* SVG figure simultaneously depicts **rectilinear light propagation in a Lobachevskian universe** and **refracted optical trajectories ($n \propto (1 - r^2)^{-1}$) in a flat Euclidean universe**!

2. The 1898–1900 Poincaré–Russell Debate (``geometor/bertrand-russell``)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Chapters III–V of *La Science et l'Hypothèse* crystallize :person:`Poincaré <poincare>`'s celebrated exchange with :person:`Bertrand Russell <russell>` (:ref:`Concordance PC.12 <concordance>`, paired with ``geometor/bertrand-russell``):

1. **Russell's *Essay* (1897)**: In :cite:`*An Essay on the Foundations of Geometry* <russell-1897>`, Russell argued that **projective geometry** is wholly *a priori* (expressing the pure form of externality), whereas constant-curvature **metrical geometry** requires an *a priori* axiom of free mobility supplemented by an empirical determination of spatial curvature.
2. **Poincaré's First Critique (*RMM* May 1899)**: In :cite:`*Des fondements de la géométrie : à propos d'un livre de M. Russell* <poincare-1899-russell>`, Poincaré challenged Russell to give an operational test distinguishing a projective straight line or equal distances without solid bodies or light rays—originating the exact argument of Chapter V § 4 (Properties $A$ and $B$ of the straight line).
3. **Russell's Reply (*RMM* Nov. 1899) & Poincaré's Rejoinder (*RMM* Jan. 1900)**: In :cite:`*Sur les axiomes de la géométrie* <russell-1899-reponse>`, Russell insisted that **distance** and **straightness** are indefinable, irreducible relations directly intuited, protesting that if all bodies doubled in size overnight the change would be real even if unmeasurable. In :cite:`*Sur les principes de la géométrie : réponse à M. Russell* <poincare-1900-replique>`, Poincaré formulated his relativity of scale (homothety invariance): an absolute spatial magnitude detached from all observable relations among bodies is a meaningless metaphysical phantom.

3. Petrus Hoenen's 1954 Critique in *De Noetica Geometriae* (``geometor/hoenen``)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Half a century later, :person:`Petrus Hoenen, S.J. <hoenen>` devoted central chapters of :cite:`*De Noetica Geometriae* <hoenen-1954>` (digitized in ``geometor/hoenen``, alongside ``geometor/aquinas`` and ``geometor/aristotle``) to evaluating Poincaré's conventionalism alongside :person:`Hilbert <hilbert>`'s formalism:

* **Why Poincaré Rightly Refuted Empiricism**: Hoenen warmly endorses Chapter V's refutation of :person:`Mill <mill>`'s geometric empiricism: physical experiments on wooden triangles and iron rods can never yield universal geometrical necessity.
* **The Blind Spot of *"Disguised Definitions"***: Hoenen argues that Poincaré was trapped by a false dichotomy—assuming that if axioms are neither Kantian innate forms nor empirical generalizations, they must be linguistic conventions. Rehabilitating Aristotelian–Thomistic abstraction in *materia intelligibilis*, Hoenen points out that **Poincaré's own Translation Dictionary proves the opposite of pure conventionalism**: to verify that Lobachevsky's axioms are free of contradiction, Poincaré must appeal to *ordinary Euclidean intuition and proofs* regarding orthogonal circles in the carrier plane! Thus intellectual judgment (*judicium*) genuinely grasps necessary nexus relations in continuous extension prior to choosing a coordinate or metric convention.

4. Toward Relativistic Spacetime (1898–1905) and General Relativity (1915)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Notice the prophetic arc of Chapter VI's four opening theses (FR pp. 111–112 / EN p. 90): by denying absolute space, absolute time, and unmediated distant simultaneity (citing his January 1898 memoir *La Mesure du Temps*, where clock synchronization by light signals is first analyzed as a convention), Poincaré laid the epistemological groundwork for his 1905 *Sur la dynamique de l'électron* and Einstein's 1905 Special Relativity. A decade later, Einstein's 1915 General Relativity answered Chapter V § 3 with a subtle twist that honored Poincaré's holism while overturning his prediction of permanent Euclidean convenience: rather than keeping flat Euclidean space plus complicated universal fifth forces deflecting both rods and light rays equally, modern physics found it simpler for the *total* system $(G \wedge P)$ to absorb gravitation into dynamical Riemannian spacetime curvature!
