Science and Hypothesis¶

La Science et l’Hypothèse — Henri Poincaré (1902 / 1905)

Author: Henri Poincaré (1854–1912)
First French Edition (1902): La Science et l’Hypothèse, Bibliothèque de Philosophie scientifique (Paris: Ernest Flammarion, 1902)
Authorized English Translations (1905): Translated by W. J. Greenstreet with Preface by Sir Joseph Larmor (London: Walter Scott, 1905), collated with the authorized American edition translated by George Bruce Halsted with Henri Poincaré's Special Preface (New York: The Science Press, 1905)
GEOMETOR Digital Edition: Synchronized English · French · Summary · Notes Tracks with Exact Symbolic Conformal Disk Models

“The geometrical axioms are therefore neither synthetic a priori intuitions nor experimental facts. They are conventions. Our choice among all possible conventions is guided by experimental facts; but it remains free, and is only limited by the necessity of avoiding every contradiction… In other words, the axioms of geometry (I do not speak of those of arithmetic) are only definitions in disguise.”

—Henri Poincaré, La Science et l'Hypothèse, Chapitre III (pp. 66–67 / EN p. 50)

Glossary

Poincaré’s term in Chapter I for mathematical induction—proving a property holds for n = 1, proving that if it holds for n - 1 it holds for n, and concluding it holds for all integers. Poincaré identifies it as the sole instrument capable of leaping from the finite to the infinite, constituting a genuine synthetic a priori intuition.
